# 2026-11-23 — P2-6: own decoder vs `<video>` elements, 30 jump cuts

The tools and method are in `bench/p26/README.md`. **"Wrong picture"** means the frame on the canvas is not the one
the timeline asks for: neither the frame at the playhead nor the nearest frame, which is what an export renders.

Each run plays 13.5 s with 30 cuts. The page (canvas) refreshes about 31 times a second. The numbers are lab numbers
on a Mac (Safari 18.6 and headless Chromium), **not yet from a real iPad**.

## Found and fixed while measuring (Safari)

1. **A Safari decoder keeps a few frames back until more input arrives.**
   - Symptom: the first frame after a jump took about 165 ms, because the decoder was only given more input after 150 ms.
   - Fix: how much extra input a file needs is remembered (`extraNeeded`). First frame p95 is now about 25 ms.
2. **Keeping more decoded frames than a hardware decoder has output pictures stalls it.**
   - Symptom: after fix 1, Safari reported "holds frames until a flush" 3 times and fell back to `<video>`.
   - Fix: the frames kept are capped separately from the extra input in flight.
3. **The last frames of each stretch stayed inside the decoder.**
   - Symptom: about 10 freezes of 100 ms per run, always just before a cut.
   - Fix: feeding goes past the stretch's end by that same extra input. Freezes went to 0.

Each fix has a unit test in `test/framesource.test.js` that fails without it.

## Follow-up (v1.14.1): still choppy on the owner's iPad, and scrubbing

**Report.** After the first deploy, the owner said the iPad was "agak better" but still choppy, and that the preview
did not move while scrubbing the timeline.

**Production log.** It showed the iPad reading the new 1 MB windows of a 102 MB light copy while that same copy was
downloaded whole, twice.

**Reproduced in the lab.** A 2-minute copy and 30 cuts spread over 113 s, streamed through a throttled proxy
(150 ms, 10 Mbps, no device copy). In Safari the decoder had:

- 128 wrong pictures;
- 14 of 30 cuts late;
- 18 freezes, up to 766 ms;
- 9 buffering stops.

### Causes found and fixed

1. **Over-fetching.** The 1 MB windows pulled the whole 18.4 MB file to play cuts that need 3.0 MB (about 150 KB per cut).
   - Byte reads are now exact ranges: one request per stretch, from the keyframe to the out-point.
   - Sound reads only its own sample runs.
   - The cuts of the next 4 s are fetched ahead in timeline order.
   - Read-ahead runs at most 3 requests at a time, and a read needed now overtakes them.
2. **Sound.** Streamed sources now use 2 s sound blocks instead of 8 s, and the bytes of upcoming blocks are fetched in parallel.
3. **Chromium's HTTP cache.** It lets one request per URL through at a time.
   - Measured at 150 ms latency: 8 parallel range reads took 1.25 s, against 0.2 s with `cache: 'no-store'`.
   - A device-copy download holds that lock, so preview reads waited behind it.
   - Safari does not serialize like this (measured: 5 reads in about 160 ms, either mode).
4. **Scrubbing over a slow network.** Every new position threw away the decode in flight, so no picture came while the finger moved.
   - A paused jump now waits (at most 1.5 s) for the picture in flight, then goes to where the finger is.
   - The groups of pictures in the direction of the finger are fetched ahead.
